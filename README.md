## Project Reflection

### Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The client was DriverPass, a business that wanted to help customers prepare for their DMV driving tests. They needed a web-based system where customers could select a training package, schedule driving lessons, access online learning materials, and take practice tests. Employees also needed to manage appointments, record lesson notes, review reports, and control account access. The system would receive DMV updates to help keep the training information current.

### What did you do particularly well?

I think I did well translating the client's interview into requirements and diagrams that explained how the system would work. I considered the responsibilities of customers, office staff, instructors, the owner, and the IT officer. I also included situations where a process might not succeed, such as insufficient package hours or an unavailable instructor or vehicle. This helped me think beyond the successful booking process.

### If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

I would revise the organization and readability of some of my UML diagrams. Connecting the actors and system functions without overcrowding the diagrams was one of the more challenging parts of the project. I would improve the spacing, shorten labels where appropriate, and route connectors more clearly. I would also review the diagrams together to make sure the same roles and processes use consistent names.

### How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I used the interview transcript to identify what each user needed to accomplish and translated those needs into requirements, use cases, and system rules. For example, the client's two-hour driving lessons and training packages informed the scheduling process and the tracking of reserved and completed hours. The request to identify who changed an appointment informed the activity history. Considering user needs is important because a system can function correctly but still fail to solve the customer's actual problem.

### How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

I approach software design by first understanding the problem, identifying the users, and breaking their needs into smaller tasks. I then use diagrams to examine both the steps in a process and the information the system needs to store. In future projects, I would ask follow-up questions earlier, check assumptions with the client, and review the diagrams alongside the written requirements. I would also use feedback and simple interface prototypes to identify confusing features before development begins.
